# Proposed science episode pipeline

**Historical proposal — superseded by [framework v2.1](editorial/README.md).** Use the revised framework and [implementation task log](implementation-v2.md) for version 2 work. The turn-level audit rules, one-minute pilot opening, model prices and spending estimates below are retained as history, not current requirements or verified runtime configuration.

This proposes the next design, not today's five-minute MVP. Every run receives the full, versioned Still Curious brief: a welcoming bedtime academic seminar with a prepared host and three complementary fictional specialists; accessible history and evidence; uncertainty and disagreement; directed follow-ups; restrained humor; and no imitation of real presenters. Users choose 5, 15, 30, or 45 minutes. The opening is one minute.

## Bounded production flow

1. **Scope and plan.** A compact model turns the user's topic and the full brief into a question, exclusions, a historical-to-current arc, and three complementary expert roles. It assigns speaking purposes, likely points of disagreement, listener follow-ups, and time by beat. Word counts derive from the selected duration (planning baseline 130 spoken words/minute), not a fixed turn count. The opening targets roughly 115–145 words. Save the outline and prompt/schema version.

2. **Research and evidence ledger.** For five minutes, run one bounded research pass. For 15–45 minutes, run two short, independent lanes in parallel: one on foundational history and how the question developed; one on current primary evidence, replications, reviews, and limits. A merger deduplicates sources and flags disagreement. Researchers discover sources with Responses `web_search`, then retrieve and read the cited paper/report/page itself. Search snippets and abstracts alone cannot support claims about results outside their coverage. Prefer primary studies for what was measured, systematic reviews or meta-analyses for the wider evidence, and authoritative institutions for stable context.

   Persist a claim ledger, not just a bibliography. Sources record stable IDs, authors/institution, date/type, DOI/canonical URL, retrieval date, and reading coverage. Save each usable fetched excerpt with an exact locator (PDF page plus section/table/figure, or HTML heading and paragraph/anchor) and content hash. Claims record wording, qualifiers, units/population, source IDs/locators, evidence type, and limits. Mark paywalled or abstract-only coverage and limit claims to what was read. Multiple summaries of one study do not count as independent corroboration. For central or disputed claims, seek an independent study/source family; disclose conflicts or lack of independence rather than implying consensus.

3. **Beat map and interactive conversation.** A planner selects ledger claims for a loose narrative arc: what was first believed or observed, what the evidence can now say, what remains uncertain, and why reasonable specialists may interpret it differently. It sets topic beats and time/word allowances by segment, without prewriting every question or answer. Give each specialist a compact role card: disciplinary lens, main curiosity, favored explanatory approach, level of certainty, one supported example/story, and one qualification or evidence-based difference of interpretation. Different roles can independently prepare short notes from the shared evidence pack; independence of drafting is not independent corroboration of facts.

   The proposed conversation uses four separately prompted speaker agents: one host and three specialists. The host sees the beat map, the conversation so far, remaining episode budget, and guest expertise, then chooses a concise question and the next speaker. The selected specialist receives its role card, relevant verified evidence and the actual preceding conversation, and supplies a response rather than a separately written monologue. The host can ask for clarification, follow an unexpected detail, invite a different interpretation, or move to the next beat. A specialist can suggest a brief qualification to another's answer; the director routes that contribution explicitly rather than running an uncontrolled debate. The next speaker reacts to the actual previous response. Bound rounds, API calls, output tokens, repeated context, and cost; stop accepting new substantive beats when it is time to conclude.

   Every factual contribution carries ledger claim IDs; factual premises in questions and anecdotes also require support. Experts have distinct, complementary lenses, not invented credentials or caricatured certainty. A brief clarification can be only a few words; a supported story or explanation can occupy a sustained turn. There is no fixed per-turn word budget or equal share of speaking time. A story follows a concrete puzzle, observation or experiment, what changed, and why it matters. Never invent firsthand experiences, historical scene details, quotations, or disagreement to make a character more entertaining. Use natural contractions, understated warmth, subject-specific wonder, and occasional dry humor; avoid generic praise and mechanical speaker rotation. Test this interactive draft against a single-writer baseline on matched evidence packs; do not assume separate prompts guarantee better dialogue.

4. **Independent fact verification.** A verifier receives the dialogue plus ledger, extracts factual assertions, and checks each against the cited fetched excerpt and locator. It uses targeted retrieval/search for material gaps, numerical statements, dates, causal language, and disputed findings; it does not merely ask the writer or researcher model to approve its own prose. It returns claim/turn IDs with `supported`, `qualify`, `replace`, or `remove`, evidence locator, and a concise reason. A claim that cannot be verified from read evidence is softened or removed. The verifier checks source identity and independence as well as whether the cited text supports the exact wording.

5. **Light conversational edit and release gates.** A separate editor trims repetition, clarifies difficult passages, and adjusts bedtime pacing while preserving speaker individuality, meaningful follow-ups, and supported factual spans/claim IDs. It assembles and trims the conversation rather than replacing it with a newly scripted exchange. It may not add claims or flatten all speakers into one voice. Compare factual spans and claim references before and after editing; route any changed factual turn back through the verifier. Deterministic checks then enforce four valid speakers, unique turn IDs, resolved claim/source IDs, valid locators, opening and total word-duration bands, required narrative beats, no uncited factual turns, no unresolved high-severity review flags, and the complete audio-character budget. Run at most one bounded repair on failed turns or structure, then rerun the same checks. If it still fails, stop and report a useful failure state; do not loop or deliver a script that failed verification.

## What the evidence pack contains

The evidence pack is the saved research supplied to the writer and verifier. It contains the bounded question; sources and reading coverage; exact supporting passages and locators; a claim ledger with qualifications, evidence type, uncertainty, and source independence; a small bank of verified experiments, historical details, and examples suitable for storytelling; and unresolved questions or claims to avoid. Its purpose is to preserve support and nuance while giving the writer concrete material. A bibliography alone is insufficient.

For example, an episode about fireflies might have a supported claim describing the light-producing reaction, its source passage and page/heading, a qualification about species or experimental conditions, and a sourced observation that can anchor a story. This is an illustrative pack structure, not research for that episode. A proposed speaker's perspective may differ in what it emphasizes, but it cannot change the supported facts.

The current hosted MVP has only a lightweight version: findings, uncertainty/limitations, three source URLs and basic locators, and claim IDs attached to dialogue. It does not yet save full source passages or run separate speaker agents. The richer pack and interactive conversation described here are future implementation work. The current writer now allows flexible turn lengths and encourages sourced storytelling and distinct voices.

## Runtime and spend controls

Following the model comparison, use GPT-6 Luna for bounded extraction, formatting, and inexpensive perspective preparation; evaluate GPT-6.1 Sol at high for speaker turns and extra-high for synthesis/verification. Use a lower effort for straightforward host routing if it meets the quality bar, instead of paying extra-high reasoning for every brief interjection. Keep model names/snapshots and reasoning effort configurable and recorded. Current published standard rates per million input/output tokens are Luna $0.10/$0.50, Sol $2/$10, and Astra $10/$50. Reasoning tokens are billed as output and actual usage depends on the task. Responses web search is $10 per 1,000 calls plus retrieved search-content tokens billed at model rates; retrieved tokens count in the input budget. Confirm rates and model availability at implementation time. Sources: [GPT-6.1 Sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6.1-sol), [pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing), and [reasoning guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning). This is a proposal; the live MVP still uses GPT-4.1 Mini for outline/research/review and GPT-4.1 for writing.

The allowances below retain the earlier single-writer comparison as a baseline, not a price estimate for the newly proposed interactive speaker-agent workflow. They are planning estimates, not measured usage, provider quotes, or guarantees, and include search, helper work, reasoning tokens, and a limited correction pass. They exclude extra speaker-agent calls, ElevenLabs, hosting, and storage. No caching, Batch/Flex discounts, or Fast/Ultrafast surcharges are assumed. Repeated evidence-pack and transcript inputs count on every call. Keep each request below the long-context premium threshold. Benchmark the interactive version separately: serial replies increase latency, and extra-high reasoning on every reply can materially increase cost.

This is an assumed five-minute workload, not a test result or a fixed relationship between effort levels. Actual outputs and reasoning may exceed these planning allowances. Choose a concrete app spend ceiling using per-stage token/search limits and reserved worst-case cost before starting each request; record measured usage before setting subscription credits. Benchmark matched topics and evidence packs to determine marginal quality gains rather than assigning invented performance percentages.

Run generation as a durable asynchronous job. Persist stage artifacts and usage; key cache entries by normalized topic, full brief/prompt version, outline, model snapshot, and research-query set. Reuse research across lengths only when topic, brief, and evidence freshness match; regenerate the beat map/script. Deduplicate concurrent requests with a stable job/stage/input hash, record provider response IDs, cap attempts/spend, and honor cancellation before new paid work. Retry only known transient failures with bounded backoff; reconcile ambiguous paid requests before retrying. Keep keys server-side and show progress, sources, cancellation, and blocked/failed states.

## Implementation hand-off

**Phase 1: evidence and contract.** Define versioned schemas for outline, source/excerpt, claim ledger, beat map, dialogue, verification, and edit diff. Implement retrieval and durable excerpt/locator capture; test source identity, missing/full text, independence, and citation resolution. Keep five-minute generation working and record per-stage usage.

**Phase 2: quality pipeline.** Add one/two-researcher routing, four role cards, bounded host-directed speaker-agent exchanges, light editing, independent targeted verification, duration bands, and the one-repair limit. A/B test against the flexible single-writer baseline with matched evidence packs. Human-review varied topics against the full brief for evidence quality, storytelling, responsive questions, distinct voices, pacing, useful disagreement, opening length, and TTS readability, including hard topics and source-access failures. Measure cost and time-to-play alongside quality.

**Phase 3: durable jobs and measured release.** Add idempotent queueing, resumable artifacts, cancellation, retry reconciliation, per-user/job spend caps, cache reuse, and progress. Validate duplicate submits, restarts, cancellation, provider timeouts/rate limits, budget exhaustion, and final script/audio duration. Release long options after measured costs and full-playback review at each length.
